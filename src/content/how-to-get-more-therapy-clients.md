## How do therapists get more clients?

Therapists get more clients by being easy to find where people look (Google search, Google Maps, and directories), easy to choose once they're found (a clear website and good reviews), known to the professionals who refer (doctors, attorneys, pastors, schools), and quick to reply when someone reaches out. Doing all four matters more than doing any one perfectly.

We call this the Full Caseload System: **get found, get chosen, get referred, get booked, and watch it work.** The rest of this guide takes each step in turn, with what to do this month.

## Why isn't my caseload full?

Most open caseloads come down to one of four gaps, and a practice can have more than one:

1. **You're hard to find.** You don't show up when someone in your area searches for your specialty, or you're not in the Google Maps results.
2. **You're hard to choose.** People find you, but your website or profile doesn't answer their real questions, or your reviews are few or low.
3. **No one's sending you people.** You rely on search and directories alone, with no referral relationships.
4. **Inquiries slip away.** People reach out, but it takes days to hear back, and they've already booked with someone else.

None of these says anything about your skill as a clinician. They're marketing gaps, and most counselors were never taught to close them.

## How do I get found on Google as a therapist?

To get found on Google, complete and maintain your Google Business Profile, give each specialty its own page on your website, and keep your practice's name, address, and phone number identical everywhere online. Google says local results are based on three factors: relevance, distance, and prominence.

According to Google, "Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results," and "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

The demand is real. In the US, "therapist near me" is searched about 381,000 times a month (Mangools KWFinder, September 2026). People searching that way usually see a map with three practices above the regular results, and many choose from those three.

**What to do:**
- Claim and complete your Google Business Profile: categories, services, hours, photos, and a description that names your specialties and area.
- Post on your profile regularly and answer common questions there.
- Build a page for each specialty you want to be known for ("anxiety counseling in [your city]"), not one "services" page with a list.
- Make sure your name, address, and phone match exactly on your website, Google profile, and directories.

Search is the slowest channel. Profile fixes can help within weeks. Rankings for competitive searches build over months.

## Is Psychology Today worth it?

For many practices, yes, as one channel. A Psychology Today listing costs $29.95 a month with no contract ([Psychology Today](https://join.psychologytoday.com/us/signup)), and it reaches a large audience. Its limit is that your profile sits beside dozens of other counselors, and you don't own the page or the traffic.

Keep it if it brings you clients. To find out whether it does, ask every new client how they heard about you. If it hasn't produced a client in several months, that $359 a year may do more elsewhere. Build the channels you own (your website, your Google profile, your referral partners) alongside it, rather than depending on it alone.

## How do I get more referrals as a therapist?

Get more referrals by building real relationships with the local professionals who already send people to counselors: primary care doctors, pediatricians, psychiatrists and other therapists, attorneys, pastors, and school counselors. Reach out with a short introduction, leave a one-page overview of your practice, follow up, and thank them.

**What to do:**
- List 20 to 30 local professionals who see the people you help best.
- Send a brief, specific introduction: who you help, how to refer, and how fast you respond.
- Offer to drop off a one-page handout for their office.
- Follow up about two weeks later if you haven't heard back, then every few months with something useful.
- Log every contact so you know where each relationship stands.

**The ethics:** the ACA Code of Ethics (A.10.b) says counselors don't give or receive commissions, rebates, or other remuneration for referrals ([ACA Code of Ethics](https://www.counseling.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/ethics/2014-aca-code-of-ethics.pdf)). A thank-you note is always appropriate; a gift card for a referral isn't. And never name a client, or confirm someone became a client, without their signed release.

## How can therapists get more Google reviews ethically?

Counselors can grow Google reviews ethically by making a passive, general invitation (such as a sign in the waiting room) and never asking clients directly. The ACA Code of Ethics (C.3.b) says counselors don't solicit testimonials from current clients, former clients, or others vulnerable to undue influence.

That rules out the usual advice to "ask every happy client." It doesn't rule out making it easy for clients who want to share their experience. A simple waiting-room sign can link to a page that explains reviewing is optional, what posting publicly means for their privacy, and that you won't respond, before linking to Google.

With this approach, Freedom Counseling Services, a group practice in Louisville, went from 3.5 to 4.0 Google stars in three weeks, without asking a single client.

**Never:** ask clients (current or former) for reviews, offer anything in exchange, ask only the clients you expect to be happy, or reply to a review in a way that confirms someone is your client. Your state board has the final word, so check its advertising rules too.

## How fast should I respond to therapy inquiries?

Respond as fast as you reasonably can, ideally the same day. Someone reaching out for counseling may contact more than one practice, and the first thoughtful reply has the advantage. At minimum, send an immediate confirmation that tells them what happens next and when.

**What to do:**
- Set up an automatic reply that confirms you received the message and says when you'll follow up (never repeat what they wrote; it may be read by someone else).
- Get an instant alert on your phone when someone reaches out.
- Keep a simple follow-up list so no inquiry waits more than a day.
- Include crisis resources (988 in the US) in any automatic reply.

## How long does it take to fill a caseload?

It depends on the channel. Referral relationships and Google Ads can bring inquiries within weeks. Google Business Profile improvements can help within weeks. Search rankings for competitive terms usually take several months. That's why the fastest-filling practices work several channels at once rather than waiting on one.

A realistic plan: fix your Google profile and website first, start referral outreach in the first month, consider ads if you need clients sooner, and let search content build in the background.

## What should a therapist website include?

A therapist website should include a page for each specialty you want to be known for, a profile for each clinician, clear answers about fit, cost, and what the first session is like, and an easy way to reach out that works well on a phone. It should also ask how people heard about you.

- **Specialty pages** written around what people search, for each specialty and location.
- **Clinician profiles** with approach, specialties, and availability.
- **Straight answers** to a nervous first-time client's questions: is this person right for me, what does it cost, do you take my insurance, what happens first.
- **An appointment request form** on your own site, not a hand-off to a confusing third-party tool.
- **Fast on mobile.** Many people search for a counselor on their phone.
- **"How did you hear about us?"** on the form, so you can measure your channels.

## How do I know which marketing is working?

Track new clients and where each came from, not website visits. Ask every inquiry "How did you hear about us?", mark which inquiries become clients, and note how long each one waited for a reply. Within a few months you'll know which channels fill your caseload and which only cost money.

**Track these four numbers monthly:**
1. New clients, and where each came from.
2. Inquiries, and the share that became clients.
3. How fast you first responded.
4. How full your caseload is (current clients against your target).

## Your 30-day plan to get more therapy clients

1. **Week 1:** Complete your Google Business Profile, and check that your name, address, and phone match everywhere.
2. **Week 1:** Add "How did you hear about us?" to your intake, and set up an automatic reply to inquiries.
3. **Week 2:** List 20 local referral partners and send your first five introductions.
4. **Week 2:** Put a passive review invitation in your waiting room.
5. **Week 3:** Build or improve one specialty page for the clients you most want to see.
6. **Week 4:** Follow up with your referral partners, and review your four numbers.
